Backup & recovery

There is no Vexunori server that holds a copy of your identity. Nobody can reset your passphrase or recover your key for you. If you lose your device, your passphrase and every backup, your identity and local data are gone.

Encrypted backups

Settings → Backup & recovery writes a .vxbackup file with everything: your secret key, contacts, messages, Vault and settings.

  • It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a backup passphrase you choose (separate from your app passphrase). Use a strong one and store it apart from the file.
  • Anyone with the file and the backup passphrase gets your whole identity.
  • Use Verify a backup to check that a saved file opens with your backup passphrase.
  • Restore on the welcome screen of a fresh install: Restore from backup. Restoring never merges into an existing profile.
  • The backup is built in your device's memory. A very large Vault (many big files) may be too large to back up on a phone; files over 512 MB are refused.
  • A backup is a snapshot. Messages after the backup are on your relays only as long as the relays keep them.

Linking another device

On the device you already use, open Settings → Security → Link another device. It shows a QR code and a one-time code. On the new device choose Link this device / import a key on the welcome screen, scan the QR and type the code. The QR contains your secret key encrypted with the one-time code, so a photo of the QR alone is not enough.

Both devices then share one identity. Each sent message is also sent to yourself, so both see the conversation going forward, plus whatever older messages your relays still keep. Contacts and settings are not synced automatically; use a backup to copy everything.

Link a device with all your history

To move everything (identity, contacts, chats, Vault, settings) to a new phone or computer without a backup file:

  1. On the new device choose Link this device on the welcome screen. It shows a QR code. The code holds only a one-time public key and the relay addresses, no secrets.
  2. On the device you already use, open Settings → Backup & recovery → Move everything to a new device without a file (or Settings → Security → Link another device), confirm with your passphrase and scan the QR code.
  3. Both screens show six words. Check they are exactly the same and confirm on both devices. If any word differs, stop: someone may have swapped the QR code.
  4. The old device sends the data; the new device shows the progress, checks the SHA-256 checksum, and asks how to protect this device (passphrase, Android Keystore or no passphrase). Then it is ready.
  • The data is the same as in a backup file. It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key from the two one-time keys (ECDH, via the NIP-44 conversation key and HKDF), split into parts, and each part is sent as a NIP-59 gift wrap to the new device's one-time key.
  • The parts go through relays. They are marked to expire after 1 hour (NIP-40), but a relay may keep them longer. Without the new device's one-time key they cannot be decrypted, and that key is deleted when the transfer ends.
  • Parts that get lost are requested again automatically. If nothing arrives for a while the transfer stops; start again on both devices.
  • Sending is paced (about 10 parts of 16 KB per second) to stay within relay limits. Above about 50 MB the app warns you: a backup file is faster for that much data.
  • Under Use a specific relay on the new device you can pick a relay on your home network (for example ws://192.168.1.10:7777) so the data never leaves your network. The old device must allow LAN relays (Nearby → LAN relay).
  • Afterwards both devices use the same identity and both receive new messages, because every message you send is also sent to yourself. The devices do not keep syncing other changes.
  • A linked device cannot be removed later. The only way to cut it off is to create a new identity and give your contacts the new invite.

Recovery kit (recovery circle)

In Settings → Backup & recovery → Recovery kit you can split your secret key into n shares for people you trust, so that any k of them together can restore it (Shamir's Secret Sharing).

  • Fewer than k shares reveal nothing about your key.
  • Any k holders together can take over your identity. Choose people who will not collude and keep shares safe.
  • Shares contain only the key — not messages or contacts.
  • Shares cannot be revoked. If too many are lost (more than n − k), recovery is impossible.

To recover, choose Recover with a recovery kit on the welcome screen of a fresh install and enter k shares.

If you lose…

…your passphrase
Your local data cannot be decrypted. Restore from a backup, a recovery kit or another linked device on a fresh install, or start over.
…your device
Restore a backup or link from another device. If the lost device had no passphrase (web device mode), whoever finds it can read your data; consider creating a new identity.
…a backup passphrase
That backup is useless. Make a new backup.
…a Space passphrase
Items in that protected Space cannot be recovered.
