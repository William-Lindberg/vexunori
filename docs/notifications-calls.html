Notifications & calls

Notifications

Vexunori uses local notifications only. There is no push service (no Firebase, no Web Push), so no push provider learns when you receive messages. The trade-off:

  • You are notified only while Vexunori is running: a browser tab is open, or the Android app is still in memory. Android may stop the app in the background at any time, after which you get nothing until you open it.
  • Workspace reminders are the one exception on Android: they are also scheduled as local notifications with the operating system, so they can fire while the app is closed (sometimes a few minutes late).
  • Notifications are off until you turn them on in Settings → Notifications and grant permission.

Preview levels:

Full
Sender and message text. On Android the lock screen still hides the text.
Sender only (default)
"New message" with the sender's name.
Hidden
"Vexunori — New message".

Whatever is shown is visible to your operating system's notification system and anyone looking at your screen. You can mute single conversations.

Calls Experimental

One-to-one voice and video calls use WebRTC. Call setup messages travel end-to-end encrypted like chat messages; the audio and video go directly between the two devices, encrypted by WebRTC (DTLS-SRTP).

  • Calls are off by default, because a direct connection reveals your IP address to the other person. Turn them on in Settings → Calls.
  • Vexunori does not run a TURN relay server. Calls work when the two devices can reach each other directly (same network, or networks that allow it). You can add STUN servers in settings to help; a STUN server learns your IP address. Behind strict firewalls or some mobile networks, calls fail.
  • Calls are refused in Tor strict mode.
  • The call screen shows the real connection state; a call is "connected" only when media actually flows.
  • Group calls are not available.
  • Calls have been tested only with simulated connections. No call between two real devices has been made yet, and some calls will fail.
