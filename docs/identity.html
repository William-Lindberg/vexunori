Your identity

Vexunori has no accounts. Your identity is a key pair created on your device: a public key that others use to reach you, and a secret key that proves you are you and decrypts your messages.

Public and secret key

  • Your public key (shown as npub1…) is your address. It is in your invite link and QR code. It is safe to share.
  • Your secret key (nsec1…) never leaves your device unless you export it. Anyone who gets it can read your messages — including old ones they stored — and send messages as you.
  • The keys are standard Nostr keys (secp256k1). Vexunori never publishes your messages publicly, but the same key could be used in other Nostr apps. We recommend using a separate key for Vexunori.

Your display name is chosen by you and is not unique or verified. Two people can use the same name; their keys always differ.

How your local data is protected

Everything Vexunori stores — your secret key, messages, contacts, Vault — is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using one random data key. That data key is protected according to the mode you choose:

You can change mode, passphrase or PIN in Settings → Security. After several wrong attempts the app makes you wait before trying again (on the lock screen and the frozen screen). This delay only slows guessing inside the app; it does not slow someone who copied the data.

Argon2id: why guessing your passphrase is slow

Your passphrase or PIN is turned into a key with Argon2id, the password hashing method recommended by RFC 9106. It is memory-hard: every single guess must fill and re-read 64 MiB of memory. Graphics cards (GPUs) and custom chips are fast at guessing because they run thousands of guesses side by side; with Argon2id each of those guesses needs its own 64 MiB, so far fewer fit at once and each one takes longer. The older method, PBKDF2, needs almost no memory per guess, which is exactly what GPUs are good at.

  • Passphrases: 64 MiB, 3 passes. PINs: 64 MiB, 4 passes. Unlocking takes a moment on purpose.
  • PINs benefit most, because they have few possibilities. Still, Argon2id only slows guessing down: a 6-digit PIN that someone copies off the device can eventually be found. Use a longer PIN, a passphrase, or PIN + Android Keystore if that matters to you.
  • If a device cannot spare 64 MiB, Vexunori uses 32 MiB instead and remembers that. It never switches to the weaker PBKDF2 for a new key.
  • Upgrade from older versions: data protected by an older version (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600 000 rounds) still opens. Right after the next successful unlock, Vexunori re-protects the same data key with Argon2id in one step; if that fails, the old protection keeps working and it tries again next time. A hidden profile is upgraded the first time you open it; until then its unlock takes a different amount of time than the others.

Strength meter

When you choose a passphrase, PIN, backup passphrase or export password, a meter rates it from 0 to 4 using zxcvbn, which knows common passwords, words, names, dates and keyboard patterns. Passphrases must reach at least 3 ("Good"). For PINs the usual PIN rules apply and the meter warns about patterns such as dates. The meter also shows a rough time to guess for someone who copied the app data, assuming about 10 000 guesses per second against our Argon2id setting — an estimate, not a promise. The word lists load only when you start typing, and what you type is never stored, logged or sent.

Auto-lock (default 15 minutes) locks the app when it is in the background or idle. Emergency Freeze in the Privacy Center locks immediately, drops keys from memory and closes connections; you need your passphrase (or an explicit "Unfreeze") to continue. It does not delete anything and cannot reach other devices.

Fingerprint or face unlock (Android) Experimental

In the Android app you can unlock Vexunori with your fingerprint or face instead of typing your PIN or passphrase. Turn it on in Settings → Security → Unlock with fingerprint; you enter your PIN or passphrase once and Android asks for your fingerprint once. The lock screen then shows Use fingerprint. It is not available on the web.

  • Your fingerprint never leaves the phone and Vexunori never sees it; Android’s secure hardware checks it and only then lets Vexunori use a key that is locked to it. That key unlocks a second copy of your data key. Your PIN or passphrase keeps working; you can always use it instead.
  • Anyone whose fingerprint is set up on the phone can use it, so only turn this on if only your fingerprints are set up.
  • If a new fingerprint is added to the phone, Android destroys the key. Vexunori then asks for your PIN or passphrase and you turn fingerprint unlock on again.
  • The fingerprint opens only your normal profile. A hidden profile always needs its own PIN or passphrase.
  • Turning it off deletes the extra copy and the key.

Hidden profile

A hidden profile is a second, separate profile on the same device with its own passphrase or PIN. Create it in Settings → Security → Hidden profile. Your normal profile needs a passphrase or PIN first, and the hidden one must use a different secret of the same kind, so the lock screen looks the same.

  • The lock screen has one field. Your normal secret opens your normal profile; the hidden secret opens the hidden one. The normal profile never lists the hidden one.
  • The hidden profile has its own identity (a new key pair), contacts, chats, Vault and settings. Add its contacts from inside the hidden profile; it shares nothing with your normal profile. Its settings start as a copy of the normal profile's settings (relays, Tor and so on).
  • Only the open profile runs: the hidden profile connects to relays and shows notifications only while it is open. To switch, lock Vexunori and type the other secret.
  • Unlocking always does the same amount of work whether or not a hidden profile exists (a decoy key derivation when there is none), so timing does not give it away. Each extra hidden profile adds one more key derivation.
  • To remove it, open it and use Delete this hidden profile. "Delete everything" removes every profile.

What it does not do: it is not undetectable. Anyone who examines the device's storage can see that a second encrypted database exists (but cannot read it without the secret). It helps against someone looking over your shoulder or making you unlock the app, not against forensic analysis of the device. Workspace reminders already scheduled as Android notifications may still appear while the other profile is open.

Export and import

  • Export your secret key from Settings as a 24-word recovery phrase, an encrypted ncryptsec (NIP-49, protected by a password you choose) or as a plain nsec. The app asks you to confirm first. A plain nsec is your whole identity in one line: store it like a password, never paste it into a website.
  • Import an nsec or ncryptsec when you create a profile to keep using an existing key.
  • To move to a new phone, prefer device linking or a full encrypted backup, which also bring your contacts and settings.

Recovery phrase (24 words)

Settings → Security → Show recovery phrase shows your secret key as 24 words from the standard BIP-39 English word list, after you type your passphrase or PIN again. Write them on paper in order, then answer a short check (words 3, 11 and 20). Each time the words are shown, a privacy receipt is logged (without the words).

Anyone with these words can take over your identity. Write them on paper; never photograph or share them.

  • To use them, choose Restore from recovery phrase on the welcome screen of a fresh install, type the 24 words (suggestions come from the word list) and choose how the device protects your data. A built-in checksum catches most typos and swapped words.
  • They bring back your identity only, not contacts, chats or settings. Use a backup for those.
  • Not a Nostr "seed phrase" (NIP-06). These words encode your key directly. Some Nostr apps turn a seed phrase into a key in a different way (NIP-06), so they will produce a different key from these words. To use your identity in another Nostr app, export your nsec or ncryptsec instead.

If your key is exposed

There is no way to "change the password" of a key. If you think your secret key leaked, create a new identity, tell your contacts the new one over a channel you trust, and ask them to verify it. Old messages encrypted to the old key remain readable to whoever has it.
